Class 7 Science
Chapter: Matter and Its Properties

1. What is Matter?

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. The chair you sit on,
the water you drink, the air inside a balloon, and the dust on a table are
all examples of matter. Light, heat, and sound are not matter because they
do not have mass and do not occupy space in the same way.

Mass tells us how much matter is present in an object. It can be measured
using a balance and is usually written in grams (g) or kilograms (kg).
Volume tells us how much space matter occupies. A measuring cylinder can be
used to measure the volume of a liquid.

2. States of Matter

Matter is commonly found in three states: solids, liquids, and gases.

Solids have a definite shape and a definite volume. The particles in a solid
are packed closely together. Examples include a pencil, a stone, an ice cube,
and a wooden desk.

Liquids have a definite volume but they do not have a definite shape. A
liquid takes the shape of the container it is placed in. The particles in a
liquid are close together but can move or slide past each other. Examples
include water, milk, cooking oil, and juice.

Gases do not have a definite shape or a definite volume. A gas spreads out
to fill the whole container. The particles in a gas are far apart and move
freely. Examples include air, oxygen, carbon dioxide, and steam.

3. Physical Properties of Matter

Physical properties are features we can observe or measure without changing
the substance into a new substance. Colour, shape, size, mass, volume, smell,
texture, and state are physical properties.

For example, when ice melts into water, it changes from a solid to a liquid,
but it is still water. This is a physical change. When water boils and becomes
steam, it changes from liquid to gas. When steam cools and becomes water
again, this change is called condensation.

4. Changes of State

Melting is the change from solid to liquid. Freezing is the change from
liquid to solid. Evaporation or boiling is the change from liquid to gas.
Condensation is the change from gas to liquid. Sublimation is the change
from solid directly to gas.

Classroom Activity

Observe a pencil, a glass of water, and air inside a balloon. Compare their
shape and volume. Decide which one is a solid, which one is a liquid, and
which one is a gas. Explain your answer using the words mass, volume, shape,
particles, solid, liquid, and gas.

Quick Questions

1. Why is air considered matter?
2. What is the difference between mass and volume?
3. Why does water take the shape of a glass?
4. What happens to particles when a liquid becomes a gas?
5. Give two examples of physical properties.
