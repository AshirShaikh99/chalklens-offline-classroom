# Product Brief

## Product Name

Product name: **ChalkLens**

Meaning:

- "Chalk" points to the blackboard, classroom, and teacher-led workflow.
- "Lens" points to the camera scan and Gemma's page understanding.

Brand note: initial web search did not show an obvious active education AI app named ChalkLens. Before a public launch, run a proper trademark, App Store, Play Store, and domain check.

## One-Line Pitch

ChalkLens turns any textbook page into a plain-English lesson, quiz, blackboard notes, and classroom activity using Gemma 4 offline.

## User

Primary user:

- Teacher in a low-resource school

Secondary users:

- Students who need simpler explanations
- Parents helping children revise
- School leaders reviewing teacher-generated materials

## Core Problem

Many schools face a combination of:

- Weak or no internet
- Large class sizes
- Limited teacher preparation time
- Textbooks written in formal or unfamiliar wording
- Students with different reading levels in the same classroom
- Lack of personalized help for weaker students
- Few digital tools that work without cloud access

The result is simple: students are present in class but still do not understand the lesson.

## Core Solution

ChalkLens gives the teacher a local AI co-pilot that can:

- Read a textbook page from a camera photo.
- Convert it into simple classroom English.
- Explain the concept in simple classroom English.
- Produce blackboard-ready notes.
- Generate oral quiz questions.
- Create a small group activity.
- Make easier and harder explanations.
- Save the lesson offline for reuse.

## Product Principle

One teacher device should help the whole classroom.

This is important because many students cannot afford expensive phones or tablets. The product should not depend on every student owning a device.

## MVP Promise

In under two minutes, a teacher can go from:

> Photo of textbook page

to:

> Complete classroom teaching kit ready for class

## Main Demo Scenario

Class 5 science lesson: the water cycle.

Teacher scans a textbook page. The app generates:

- "Evaporation" explained using wet clothes drying in sunlight.
- "Condensation" explained using water droplets on a cold glass.
- Blackboard notes in simple English.
- Oral quiz in simple English.
- One classroom activity using a bowl of water, sunlight, and observation.
- Easier explanation for struggling students.
- Key-terms glossary: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection.

## What Success Looks Like

The judge should understand within 30 seconds:

- Who has the problem.
- Why current AI tools do not solve it well.
- Why local Gemma 4 matters.
- How the app helps a real teacher tomorrow.
